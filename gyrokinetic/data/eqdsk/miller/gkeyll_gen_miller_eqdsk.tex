\section{Purpose, inputs, and conventions}
The module \texttt{gkeyll\_gen\_miller\_eqdsk.py} constructs a synthetic
axisymmetric magnetic geometry for the existing Gkeyll tokamak reader.
Prescribed surfaces and safety factor determine poloidal flux, with constant
$F=RB_\phi$. These choices do not in general solve the Grad--Shafranov equation.
The GEQDSK pressure, pressure derivative, and current fields are zero
placeholders; the physical wall is not specified.

Lengths are in meters and magnetic fields in tesla. The flux $\psi$ is poloidal
flux divided by $2\pi$, in Wb/radian. We set $\psi_{\rm axis}=0$, with positive
$B_{\rm axis}$ and $q$ and flux increasing outward. The input function
$q(\Romp)$ takes the major radius at the outboard midplane, in meters.
The Miller surface label $r$ is a minor radius, whereas $R$ is the cylindrical
major radius at an arbitrary poloidal position.

Required parameters are $\Ra$, $B_{\rm axis}$, and the outboard LCFS major
radius. Defaults are $\Za=0$, $\kappa=1$, $\delta=0$, and $A=0$, where
$A$ denotes \texttt{a\_shift}. Optional outboard midplane bounds identify
complete flux surfaces intended for a simulation. They are independent of
the rectangular mesh bounds and do not crop the equilibrium to an annulus.
Core annuli, SOL-only regions, and LCFS-crossing regions are all possible.
Miller surfaces themselves contain no diverted X-point; the IWL infrastructure
supplies wall cuts for the open-field-line portion of a simulation.

\section{Surface map and analytic derivatives}
Define $\eta=\arcsin\delta$ and $h=\theta+\eta\sin\theta$. Then
\begin{align}
R(r,\theta)&=\Ra-\frac{Ar^2}{2\Ra}+r\cos h, &
Z(r,\theta)&=\Za+\kappa r\sin\theta,\\
R_r&=-\frac{Ar}{\Ra}+\cos h, &
R_\theta&=-r(1+\eta\cos\theta)\sin h,\\
Z_r&=\kappa\sin\theta, & Z_\theta&=\kappa r\cos\theta.
\end{align}
Let $D=R_rZ_\theta-R_\theta Z_r$. The cylindrical volume Jacobian is
$J_r=RD$. A useful regular expression is
\begin{equation}
\frac{D}{r}=\kappa\left[
\left(\cos h-\frac{Ar}{\Ra}\right)\cos\theta
+(1+\eta\cos\theta)\sin h\sin\theta\right].
\end{equation}
The implementation checks positive $R$ and $D/r$ on sampled physical surfaces,
including the limiting value at the axis. It also checks that the outboard
midplane map remains increasing. These numerical checks cannot prove validity
of an arbitrary user function between all sampled points.

At $\theta=0$,
\begin{equation}
\Romp(r)=\Ra-\frac{Ar^2}{2\Ra}+r,\qquad
\frac{d\Romp}{dr}=1-\frac{Ar}{\Ra}.
\end{equation}
For $d=\Romp-\Ra$, its stable increasing-branch inverse is
\begin{equation}
r=\frac{2d}{1+\sqrt{1-2Ad/\Ra}}.
\end{equation}
This expression includes the $A=0$ limit without cancellation. The LCFS
radius $a$ is obtained by applying it to the specified LCFS major radius.
A nonzero shift changes the horizontal surface center with radius; it is
independent of the constant vertical offset $\Za$.

\section{Flux, safety factor, and magnetic field}
Set $F=\Ra B_{\rm axis}$. In cylindrical coordinates the field convention is
\begin{equation}
B_R=-\frac{\psi_Z}{R},\qquad B_Z=\frac{\psi_R}{R},\qquad B_\phi=\frac{F}{R}.
\end{equation}
Because $\psi=\psi(r)$,
\begin{equation}
\psi_R=\psi'(r)\frac{Z_\theta}{D},\qquad
\psi_Z=-\psi'(r)\frac{R_\theta}{D},\qquad
B_p=\frac{\psi'(r)}{RD}\sqrt{R_\theta^2+Z_\theta^2}.
\end{equation}
The toroidal turns per poloidal circuit give
\begin{equation}
q(r)=\frac{F}{2\pi\psi'(r)}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{D}{R}\,d\theta.
\end{equation}
Consequently the imposed safety factor determines
\begin{equation}
\psi'(r)=\frac{F}{2\pi q(\Romp(r))}
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{D}{R}\,d\theta.
\end{equation}
The boundary flux is $\psi_b=\psi(a)$, and
$\rho_\psi=\sqrt{\psi/\psi_b}$, including values above one in the physical SOL.
The profile must be defined from the axis through the guard, even when the
simulation begins away from the axis.

\section{Radial quadrature and interpolation}
Use $s=r^2$. With $g(s)=d\psi/ds$, the regular quadrature is
\begin{equation}
g(s)=\frac{F}{4\pi\Ra q(\Romp(\sqrt{s}))}
\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\Ra}{R}\frac{D}{r}\,d\theta.
\end{equation}
The integrand is dimensionless and has a finite limit at $s=0$. A constant
triangularity extended to the axis can introduce $\sqrt{s}$ terms into $g$;
finiteness does not imply an analytic Taylor series in $s$ or a smooth axis
Hessian of $\psi(R,Z)$ for general triangular shapes.

SciPy adaptive quadrature evaluates the integral with relative and
dimensionless absolute tolerances $0.05\epsilon$, where the default
$\epsilon=10^{-10}$. Estimated errors above
$\epsilon\max(1,|I|)$ are rejected. All evaluated $q$ and $g$ must be finite
and positive. A 65-node initial table uses $s_i=s_g(i/64)^4$ to cluster near
the axis. A monotone piecewise cubic Hermite interpolant (PCHIP) represents
$g$. Independent midpoint quadratures identify intervals for bisection;
quadratures at unchanged locations are cached. Midpoint relative error is
measured against $\max(|g|,10^{-8}\max_i g_i)$. Refinement stops when every
midpoint error is at most $\epsilon$, with limits of 120 passes and 262145
nodes. Failure to converge is reported, rather than reducing accuracy silently.

Integrating the piecewise cubic gives a continuous flux function. Positivity
of the shape-preserving interpolant makes flux strictly increasing off the
axis. Bracketed Brent solves in $s$ invert flux, with relative tolerance
$10^{-14}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-14}s_g$. The inverse is restricted
to the physical interval. The axis has $\psi'(0)=0$.

\section{Inverting the R--Z map}
For a requested physical point set
\begin{equation}
v=(Z-\Za)/\kappa,\qquad u=r\cos\theta,\qquad
r=\sqrt{u^2+v^2},\qquad \beta=\eta v/r.
\end{equation}
The scalar equation is
\begin{equation}
R=\Ra-\frac{A(u^2+v^2)}{2\Ra}+u\cos\beta-v\sin\beta.
\end{equation}
At the origin the ratio $v/r$ is defined as zero. For $r>0$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_u=-\eta vu/r^3,\qquad
R_u=\cos\beta-Au/\Ra-(u\sin\beta+v\cos\beta)\beta_u.
\end{equation}
Symmetric brackets start at $u=\pm r_g$ and expand by a factor of 1.5 as
needed, at most 12 times. The derivative is checked at 65 locations across
each bracket; a nonpositive derivative rejects a folded branch. Fifty
vectorized bisections solve the equation. Recover $\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(v,u)$
and verify the forward residual is at most $10^{-11}\Ra$.
No scattered interpolation or nearest-point fill is used.

\section{Coverage and rectangular continuation}
The outer required radius is $\max(a,r_{\rm requested,hi})$, or $a$ if bounds
are omitted. The physical guard is $r_g=(1+f)r_{\rm required}$, with $f=0.05$
by default. Automatic rectangular bounds enclose the guard contour with
two percent of its horizontal/vertical span added on each side. Explicit
bounds must contain the axis and have positive minimum major radius.

For $s>s_g=r_g^2$ continue the flux as
\begin{equation}
\psi(s)=\psi(s_g)+g(s_g)(s-s_g).
\end{equation}
This matches flux and its first radial derivative and stays increasing.
It supplies numerical background at rectangular corners; it does not certify
physical flux surfaces outside the guard or require q quadrature there.
The analytic coordinate inverse must remain valid at background points.

Measure distance in units of R and Z mesh spacings. Sampled LCFS and outer
required contours must be at least three cells from the guard and box edges.
Guard distances use a nearest-neighbor tree on 4097 guard points. Insufficient
clearance fails with instructions to refine the mesh or enlarge the guard/box.
All fields, certified bounds, background-node counts, and numerical settings
are recorded in the JSON companion. Optional coverage endpoints are reported
in all four radial coordinates.

\section{GEQDSK serialization}
The header uses a deterministic 48-character label followed by three I4
integers. Arrays use five E16.9 values per line; a partially filled final line
is terminated before the next array. Odd node counts from 5 to 999 are accepted.
The flux array has Z as its outer index and R as its inner (fastest) index.
The four one-dimensional profiles are $F$, zero pressure, zero $FF'$, and
zero pressure derivative. QPSI is evaluated at uniform flux nodes between zero
and $\psi_b$ by inverting the physical radial table. A 257-point closed LCFS
polyline is written as alternating $(R,Z)$ pairs. LIMITR is zero. A temporary
file in the output directory is closed successfully before replacing the
destination. JSON records input provenance and the conventions above.

\section{Examples and verification}
The DIII-D and TCV examples retain the source input's q polynomials, field
normalization, and shaping. TCV's input coordinate is linear in Miller radius;
its exact surfaces have outboard endpoints approximately 1.051359 and
1.155593 m. These differ from its nominal outboard bounds. TCV must be read
with reflection disabled to preserve its vertical offset.

The original LTX rational q has a pole at about 0.688328 m, inside its old
sampled range. The replacement is
\begin{equation}
q(r)=q_a\exp\left[\frac{\hat s_a}{2}\left(\frac{r^2}{a^2}-1\right)\right].
\end{equation}
It preserves $q(a)=q_a$, $[r q'/q]_{a}=\hat s_a$, positivity, and $q'(0)=0$.

The original LTX files remain available for comparison:
\begin{itemize}
\item \path{gyrokinetic/data/eqdsk/write_efit_ltx_miller.py}
\item \path{gyrokinetic/data/eqdsk/ltx_miller.geqdsk}
\end{itemize}

For circular, unshifted, constant-q surfaces the independent reference is
\begin{equation}
\psi(r)=\frac{F}{q}\left[\Ra-\sqrt{\Ra^2-r^2}\right]
=\frac{F}{q}\frac{r^2}{\Ra+\sqrt{\Ra^2-r^2}}.
\end{equation}
Tests compare this flux and its inverse to normalized tolerance $10^{-8}$,
check analytic derivatives by finite differences, and read output with an
independent fixed-width parser and Gkeyll's EFIT reader. Rectangular refinement
uses 65, 129, and 257 nodes with sufficient guards. An independent tensor cubic
interpolant reconstructs $\nabla\psi$, $B_p$, and $B$. Contour safety factor is
checked using
\begin{equation}
q=\frac{F}{2\pi}\oint\frac{dl}{R^2B_p}.
\end{equation}
The target relative field and q error on the device simulation regions is
$10^{-3}$. Physical contours are compared before coordinate-dependent metrics.
Failure tests include invalid profiles, folds, inadequate coverage, malformed
inputs, and the original LTX pole. Run the documented tests to obtain current
results; no turbulence simulation is needed for this generator validation.

\subsection{Recorded validation}
The initial CPU validation on 21 October 2026 used $257\times257$ R--Z
meshes and the supplied device inputs. Maximum errors on sampled simulation
surfaces, using the independent tensor cubic reconstruction, were:

       & $|\Delta q|/q$ \\
\hline
LTX   & $8.0\,10^{-9}$ & $1.8\,10^{-8}$ & $2.9\,10^{-6}$ & $5.5\,10^{-7}$ \\
DIII-D & $6.2\,10^{-9}$ & $1.6\,10^{-8}$ & $2.3\,10^{-6}$ & $4.3\,10^{-7}$ \\

Native C tests passed for generated C and Python equilibria, including EFIT

and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer; the linked Gkeyll library was not instrumented.
Existing LTX geometry and EFIT tests passed. Serial and two-rank MPI app tests
initialized and advanced core-only, SOL-only, and crossing domains and checked

\section{Native C updater and gyrokinetic app integration}
\subsection{Updater interface}
The implementation and public header reside in \path{gyrokinetic/zero/}:
\path{gen_miller_eqdsk.c} and \path{gkyl_gen_miller_eqdsk.h}. Functions with prefix
\texttt{gkyl\_gen\_miller\_eqdsk} provide the following operations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{\_inew}: construct the generator and radial flux table.
\item \texttt{\_advance}: sample and validate the rectangular mesh.
\item \texttt{\_write}: atomically write the sampled GEQDSK file.
\item \texttt{\_get\_info}: return flux and coverage metadata.
\item \texttt{\_psi\_from\_omp} and \texttt{\_omp\_from\_psi}: convert
  between outboard major radius and physical flux.
\item \texttt{\_surface}: evaluate the Miller surface map.
\item \texttt{\_release}: free the generator.
\end{itemize}
The \texttt{qprofile} callback has arguments
\texttt{(double R\_omp, void *ctx)}. This updater runs on the host during
initialization and has no CUDA or MPI dependency.

The native implementation uses the same radial model and PCHIP antiderivative.
Its angular integration doubles a periodic trapezoidal rule from 32 through
16384 points, accepting successive integrals when their difference is at most
$0.05\,\mathrm{rtol}\max(1,|I|)$. It checks guard distances by direct search over
the same 4097 samples and performs scalar bisections for rectangular inversion.

\subsection{App inputs and coordinate conversion}
Select Miller geometry by setting \texttt{geometry.geometry\_id}
to \texttt{GKYL\_GEOMETRY\_MILLER}. Supply parameters through
\texttt{geometry.miller\_grid\_info} of type
\texttt{struct gkyl\_gen\_miller\_eqdsk\_inp}. In this mode, the app's
\texttt{lower[0]} and \texttt{upper[0]} are outboard major radii in meters.
Initialization resolves the mutable input \emph{in place} to $\psi$ bounds
and \texttt{GKYL\_GEOMETRY\_TOKAMAK} before constructing the configuration grid.
Solver callbacks subsequently receive the ordinary tokamak coordinates
$(\psi,\alpha,\text{parallel coordinate})$, rather than the analytic input's
$(x,y,\theta)$. Use the updater conversions when migrating physical profiles.
Binormal extents and parallel coordinates follow the existing tokamak
conventions; analytic-input callbacks require the corresponding coordinate
transformations.

The output is \texttt{<app-name>-miller.geqdsk} unless
\texttt{geometry.efit\_info.filepath} is set. Rank zero writes the file;
collective status handling and synchronization precede reading it. The file
is retained, and generation parameters are recorded in geometry metadata.
The $q$ callback must be deterministic and identical on all ranks.
Initialization regenerates the file, including before a restart; there is no
cached-file reuse mode. Keep the same geometry and $q$ inputs when restarting.

The app selects IWL geometry, disables reflection, and derives the R--Z search
bounds. In particular, \texttt{reflect=false} preserves TCV's vertical offset.
The current interface supports single-block 2x/3x domains that exclude the
coordinate singularity at the magnetic axis. Multiblock and language-binding
APIs are not provided. Check the returned app pointer for \texttt{NULL} after
a Miller initialization failure.

An LCFS crossing sets \texttt{has\_LCFS} and must align with a $\psi$ cell face.
Requested domain surfaces are not moved; a mismatch beyond GEQDSK serialization
precision causes initialization to fail. The value \texttt{x\_LCFS} is represented
by the matching grid face, while the wall cutoff uses the serialized boundary
flux. Core-only and SOL-only domains have no internal split. Set ordinary
species boundary conditions: twist-shift for a 3x core annulus and the desired
open-line conditions for SOL/crossing cases. The existing mixed-domain
machinery handles the core part of a crossing.

\subsection{Native unit tests and app regression}
From the repository root, build and run the native unit tests with:
\begin{verbatim}
make -j6 LIBRARY_TARGET=library gyrokinetic
make -j6 build/gyrokinetic/unit/ctest_gen_miller_eqdsk
./build/gyrokinetic/unit/ctest_gen_miller_eqdsk
\end{verbatim}
Use no more than half the available processors for \texttt{make}, reducing
\texttt{-j6} as needed. Native tests construct temporary fixtures, so normal
unit runs require no Python. To also exercise the native reader on Python
outputs, generate \texttt{miller\_ltx.geqdsk}, \texttt{miller\_d3d.geqdsk}, and
\texttt{miller\_tcv.geqdsk} in one directory and set
\texttt{GKYL\_MILLER\_REFERENCE\_DIR} to that directory when running the test.
Both quadratic and cubic reconstructions are checked; full geometry
construction uses the existing quadratic tokamak path.

The regression \path{gyrokinetic/creg/rt_gk_miller_3x2v_p1.c} initializes core-only,
SOL-only, and LCFS-crossing domains and advances each by one step. Constant
profiles and a static zero field isolate geometry and boundary-condition
initialization. MPI partitions the third configuration (parallel) direction:
\begin{verbatim}
make -j6 build/gyrokinetic/creg/rt_gk_miller_3x2v_p1
./build/gyrokinetic/creg/rt_gk_miller_3x2v_p1 -s1
<MPI-prefix>/bin/mpirun -np 2 \
  ./build/gyrokinetic/creg/rt_gk_miller_3x2v_p1 -M -e2 -s1
\end{verbatim}
These commands assume the repository root. To contain output in a scratch
directory, run the executable by its absolute path and ensure that the runtime
can locate the Gkeyll shared library.
